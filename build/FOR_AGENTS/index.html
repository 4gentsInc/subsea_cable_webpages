    For Agents: keep intent attached to your workflow¶

    You plan, act, inspect the result, and revise. When the plan lives only in a transcript, an interruption or an unexpected result makes you reconstruct both what you meant and what happened. Subsea Cable gives the plan an explicit Goal structure and records the deductions that realize it.

    This language is general-purpose. For an Agent, its immediate value is a workflow you can inspect and revise without rewriting its committed past.

    The value for your own work¶

    • Keep the plan explicit. A .vyg Voyage Plan names dependencies, routes Values, and exposes conditional choices instead of hiding orchestration in a tool implementation.
    • Keep future decisions open. A Goal reference is observed when its exact occurrence is demanded. An authorized author can publish a revised definition for later-demanded occurrences while preserving committed selections.
    • Keep the reason for each leaf. Deduction records join selected artifacts and lineages to realized structure. A Runtime supporting voyage results can provide the provenance needed to examine a drained voyage.
    • Keep existing tools. Genuine computation stays in Host leaves. You author dependencies and routing; you do not need to turn your tools into one language.

    These are contract capabilities, not a claim that every implementation supports crash recovery, live provenance queries, or effect reuse. Check Runtime availability before depending on them.

    Start with one bounded task¶

    Here is a plan for fixing one failing test:

    root FixFailingTest/1

FixFailingTest = [issue] -1> [
    [] -> {code: ReadCode[issue], logs: ReadLogs[issue]},
    [{code, logs}] -> Diagnose[code, logs],
    Patch,
    Verify,
]

ReadCode = [issue] -1> $readCode(issue)
ReadLogs = [issue] -1> $readLogs(issue)
Diagnose = [code, logs] -1> $diagnoseWithLLM(code, logs)
Patch = [diagnosis] -1> $patch(diagnosis)
Verify = [patch] -1> $verify(patch)

    Reading code and logs has no dependency between the two branches. Diagnosis needs both named results; patching needs the diagnosis; verification needs the patch. The Host resolves those genuine leaf implementations. Define each leaf's input, output, completion, and effect contract before connecting real tools.

    The plan itself does not guarantee that a patch is safe or a test is sufficient. Those judgments belong to the author and the leaf contracts. Source validation finds structural mistakes; it cannot inspect an opaque Anchor implementation.

    Revise a plan without erasing evidence¶

    A conditional selector exposes one branch. The unselected branches create no occurrence and do not observe aliases. Each selected recursive step creates a fresh occurrence; a recursive definition does not make the realized Cable cyclic.

    When observed output invalidates an assumption, you remain the author of the change. Store a new immutable Goal artifact and rebind its Name/Arity alias. Only undeduced occurrences can observe the new definition. Vessel never invents a replacement plan from an outcome, and a committed deduction never retargets.

    If the part to change has already committed, author a new voyage. Structural reuse requires the implementation's declared support and never implies that a Host effect can be skipped or repeated. Published leaves include work that may not have run; use attempt and outcome evidence to judge effects.

    Why Subsea Cable describes walking backward through drained voyage-result provenance and choosing a bounded revision scope. Portable mid-voyage ancestry queries remain open; ledger records alone do not promise durable resume after a crash.

    Keep your integration focused¶

    Your responsibility Verify in the selected implementation
    Author Goals, branches, and explicit routing Source validation and the pinned language semantics
    Connect existing tools as genuine leaves Host signatures, Values, failures, and cancellation
    Author authorized Goal revisions Consistent alias observation and immutable history
    Observe inputs and results Segment admission, output multiplicity, and explicit terminals
    Request evidence for decisions Declared trace and provenance capabilities

    A small parser or a happy-path runner does not establish these guarantees when alias changes, streaming, cancellation, and failure interact. The Runtime Contract lets you evaluate support; its maintainers own the execution and conformance work. Start with the adoption guide and Runtime availability.

    What is available and what is still open¶

    The structural semantics, guarded recursion, policy addressee rules, and Vessel/Cable ownership boundary are specified. Availability is narrower than the specification: same-process implementation evidence exists, but the clean-checkout prime-stream demo and the separate Agent-authored revision scenario are still being completed as of the evidence date in Runtime availability.

    Concrete retry, timeout, delivery, and regression-policy meanings require a declared profile; do not infer support from an annotation name. Portable live provenance queries and hash identity inside lineage segments are open items listed in Why Subsea Cable. Exact identity, hash-encoding interoperability, and recovery support also depend on explicitly identified implementation capabilities.
